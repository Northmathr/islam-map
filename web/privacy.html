What is collected

WhatWhy
Your email addressTo send the alerts. There is no other way.
The councils you choseTo know which applications concern you. Stored as GSS codes.
The date and time you signed up, your IP address and browser string The record that consent was given, which UK GDPR requires to be demonstrable rather than merely asserted.
Which applications you have been sent So the same one is never sent twice. Stored as a reference number against your row, never the message itself.

Nothing else. No name, no location beyond the councils you pick, no tracking pixel in the emails, no analytics on this site, and no attempt to work out anything about you from your choices.

The lawful basis is your consent

You ask for these emails and confirm by clicking a link, which is why nothing is sent until you do. An unconfirmed request is deleted after seven days. You can withdraw consent at any time, and withdrawing it is as easy as giving it.

Unsubscribing deletes your address

Every email carries an unsubscribe link, and your mail client’s own unsubscribe button works too. Using either deletes your row — address, councils, consent record and send history — rather than flagging it as suppressed. Many senders keep a suppression list at that point; this does not, because the request was to be gone.

The consequence is worth stating: if you unsubscribe by mistake, there is nothing to restore and you would sign up again.

Who else sees it

  • The email provider handles delivery, so it necessarily processes your address, and keeps its own delivery logs.
  • The database host stores the table.
  • The site host runs the sign-up, confirm and unsubscribe endpoints.

Nobody is sent your address for any other purpose. It is not sold, shared, or used to build a profile, and the list is not published in any form — including as a count broken down in a way that could identify a small council’s subscribers.

How long it is kept

  • Unconfirmed sign-ups: seven days, then deleted.
  • Confirmed subscriptions: until you unsubscribe.
  • Send history: deleted with your subscription.
  • Addresses that hard-bounce repeatedly are removed, because an address that does not exist is not a subscriber.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask for a copy of what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected, ask for it to be deleted, and complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office. Given how little is stored, deletion is the unsubscribe link and a copy is your address plus the councils you chose.

Security

The subscriber table has row level security enabled with no policy granting public access, so the key shipped to the browser cannot read it — only the server-side endpoints can. Unsubscribe links are signed rather than guessable. Nothing about subscribers is committed to the project’s public repository.
